Non-negotiables

Five rules I don't bend.

Nothing requires an app.

A convenience, never a dependency. App-only features don't exist here.

Every device keeps a physical control.

A switch where a switch belongs, still working when the network isn't.

A guest should never know.

They use your house like any other, and you explain nothing.

Everything runs locally.

On a computer in your house. Nobody else's outage becomes your problem.

Full control from anywhere.

A hotel room in another timezone works like your own kitchen.

The first three are the hard ones. Most of this industry quietly breaks all three.

When the parts can talk

Your lights can answer for your air quality.

No single device does anything interesting. The good part starts when everything lives in one system and can be wired to anything else.

Twenty minutes before your alarm

Lights rise so slowly you can't catch them moving, curtains part an inch at a time. Or the same sequence at full blast with heavy metal — the house has no opinion about which is correct.

The mailbox door opens

The nearest speaker says you've got mail, so nobody walks down to an empty box in February.

CO₂ climbs at your desk

The desk lamp pulses amber twice, caught in the corner of your eye. Ignore it and the house turns your music down and says so out loud.

The freezer starts warming up

Every speaker in the house says so, and keeps saying so. The failure that costs the most is the one nothing announces.

The sun hits the west windows

The shades close before the room gets hot. You'll notice about once — the day you realize four o'clock stopped being unbearable.

You feel like it

How you control it

Four ways to control the house. One is doing nothing.

A switch on the wall, any screen you own, or your voice — and most of the time, none of them.

Most of the time

You don't

The house watches who's home, the daylight, the damp and the hour, and handles the obvious cases.

To change something

A switch on the wall

Real dimmers and buttons where switches have always been, working the same for everyone.

When you want detail

Any screen you own

Phone, tablet, laptop or browser — nothing to install, and an interface built for each room.

When your hands are full

Your voice

Running entirely on a computer in your house. It is not Alexa, and that is the entire point.
